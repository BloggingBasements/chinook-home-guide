Fall Prep

Autumn Leaves and Your Home's Foundation

By Rob Ferland  ·  October 1, 2026  ·  4 min read

Leaves look harmless enough when they are scattered across the grass, but they behave differently once they get wet and clump together. A thick mat of sodden leaves piled up against your foundation wall is like holding a wet sponge against the concrete. It keeps that area from drying out, especially in the shorter, cooler days of fall. This constant moisture saturates the soil right next to your home's footing. Over time, that water will look for a way in. It can work on tiny, existing shrinkage cracks in the concrete and make them larger. When the deep freeze arrives, the trapped moisture in the soil expands and puts pressure on your foundation walls. This freeze and thaw cycle has a lot of force, and wet soil next to the house magnifies its effect. A few hours with a rake can prevent problems that are harder to deal with later.

A Methodical Cleanup Around the House

When you head out to clear the leaves, treat the job as more than tidying the lawn. Your main focus should be the perimeter of your house. You want a clean, dry strip about two or three feet wide all the way around the foundation. This is where the work matters most. While you are there, pay special attention to a few areas that often get missed.

  • Window Wells: These are basically buckets set into the ground against your foundation. If they fill with leaves, they will also fill with water, which then has nowhere to go but against your basement window and its frame. Scoop them out completely.
  • Under Decks: Leaves like to blow under low decks and stairs. This hidden pile can hold moisture against the house and can also create a home for pests.
  • Gutters and Downspouts: If your gutters are full of leaves, the next rain will send water over the sides and dump it right next to the foundation. This defeats their purpose. Make sure gutters are clear and that your downspouts are directing water well away from the house.

Clearing these spots is just as important as raking the open lawn. It is all part of managing water before it has a chance to reach the foundation.

Checking Your Grade

Once the leaves are cleared away, you have a good chance to inspect the ground itself. The soil around your foundation should have a gentle, positive slope away from the walls. This is called the grade. It does not have to be steep, but it should be obvious. The idea is to encourage rain and meltwater to move away from your house, not toward it. With the ground bare, you can see any low spots or areas where the ground has settled and now slopes back toward the foundation. These are places where water will pool. I remember inspecting a newer home where the builder had done a poor job of backfilling. Every time it rained, a puddle formed against the back wall. The homeowner had a persistent damp smell in his basement, and it was all because of a simple grading issue. If you find low spots, you can often fix them yourself by adding some compacted soil to build the area back up. It is a straightforward chore that can make a noticeable difference.

What to Do If You Find a Crack

Sometimes, when you clear away the leaves and dirt from the foundation, you find a crack in the concrete. The first thing to do is not to panic. Most concrete foundations have a few small hairline cracks that appear as the concrete cures and settles. These are usually not a structural concern. The ones that need more attention are wider cracks, long horizontal cracks, or cracks that are wider at the top than the bottom. Also, look for any white, powdery mineral deposits around a crack, which shows that water has been passing through it. If you discover a significant crack or have ongoing issues with water in your basement despite your best efforts, it might be time to call in a professional. The Final Walk Before Winter

Fall cleanup is really about preparing the house for the long winter ahead. It is a slow, methodical mix of observation and maintenance. After you have raked the leaves, cleaned the gutters, and checked the grade, take one last walk around the property. Make sure your exterior hose bibs are shut off. This is part of the same mindset as draining garden equipment; it is all about controlling water before it can freeze and cause damage. Check that vents for the furnace and hot water tank are clear of any debris. A little time spent in the autumn can give you some peace of mind and help your home face a Calgary winter. It feels better than just having a neat lawn.
