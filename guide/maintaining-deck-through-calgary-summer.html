Summer and Hail

Deck Maintenance for a Calgary Summer

By Rob Ferland  ·  September 22, 2026  ·  5 min read

We wait a long time for summer in this part of the world. When it arrives, most people want to be outside as much as possible, and for many Calgary homeowners that means time on the deck. A deck works like an extra room, but it has a harder life than anything indoors. It takes everything our climate throws at it, from high-altitude sun to sudden downpours and hail. A bit of steady maintenance each year keeps the deck safer for your family and helps it last. If you ignore it, it starts to look tired and can move into repairs or even replacement sooner than you'd like.

The Annual Inspection

Before getting out cleaner or stain, start with a slow inspection. Treat it as an annual checkup. The first place to look is where the deck ties into the house. This board is called the ledger, and it carries a lot of weight. Check for gaps between the ledger and the house, and look for rot or water staining on the siding right below it. The fasteners here should be heavy bolts or proper structural screws. Make sure they are tight and not rusted through. This connection is where I've seen the most serious failures over the years.

Next, look at the posts and support beams underneath. Pay close attention to the bottoms of the posts where they meet the ground or concrete footings. That area stays damp and tends to rot first. I carry a plain screwdriver and gently poke the wood. If it feels soft or spongy instead of firm, that needs attention. Then walk the whole deck surface. Notice if any areas feel extra bouncy or soft underfoot. Look along the deck boards for splitting, warping, or decay. Check the stairs for movement and the handrails for strength. Grab each railing post and give it a solid shake. A good railing barely moves.

Cleaning is More Than Tidying

Once you understand the condition of the structure, move on to cleaning. This is more than sweeping off leaves. For pressure-treated or cedar decks, use a deck cleaning solution. These cleaners are made to deal with mildew and algae and to lift out ground-in dirt. I usually tell people to be careful with pressure washers unless they really know how to use one. Too much pressure chews up the wood fibres and leaves a fuzzy, splintered surface that is hard to put right. A stiff-bristled brush on a pole and some patient scrubbing is often safer and cleans well. For composite decks, cleaning is usually simpler. Mild soap and water is often enough, but follow the manufacturer's instructions if you still have them. The idea is to remove the grime that holds moisture and encourages mold, even on synthetic materials.

To Seal, To Stain, or To Leave It

With a clean, inspected wood deck, the next choice is how to protect it. The main problems for exterior wood are water and sunlight. A decent stain or sealer helps with both. A clear water sealer helps shed rain but gives little protection from fading and sun damage. A semi-transparent stain adds colour and gives good protection from the sun while still showing the grain. A solid stain covers the grain completely and looks more like paint. It offers the most sun protection and can hide marks in older wood, but it can peel if the prep or application is poor. Whatever product you use, the deck should be fully dry first. In a Calgary summer that can mean waiting a few days after cleaning or a rain for the moisture to leave the wood.

A Note on Fasteners and Hardware

During your inspection, you likely noticed some of the fasteners that hold the deck together. If you see nails that have popped up, don't just hammer them back down. A popped nail usually means the wood has expanded and shrunk enough times that the nail has lost its grip. A better approach is to pull the nail and replace it with a deck screw, which holds far more securely. If you find loose bolts on railings or support beams, tighten them. If a bolt just spins and never snugs up, the wood around it may have rotted or crushed and lost strength.

I was in a home in Altadore once where the owner complained about a real bounce in his deck. He said he hammered the nails back in every spring. I showed him how the wood fibres no longer gripped the nail shafts after years of movement. We pulled a few nails and replaced them with proper deck screws. The whole structure felt more solid right away.

Dealing With Summer Storms

Summer work on a house is different from winter prep. In January, the focus is on keeping things from freezing solid. I've written before about how to keep your garage door working when the temperature really drops, but summer brings strong sun and the odd hailstorm. After a good hailstorm moves through your street, it is worth inspecting the deck surface. Hail can leave dents and pockmarks in wood. Every little mark is a place where the stain or sealer may have cracked, giving water an easy way into the wood and starting rot. Some light sanding and touch-up stain or sealer in those spots can close things up again. Composite decking handles hail better, but a heavy storm can still leave cosmetic marks.

Looking after a deck is not a single afternoon's chore. Taken one step at a time, it is manageable for most homeowners. A weekend of work in early summer can set things up for months of use. It protects the structure and gives your family a safe, solid place to sit outside when the warm weather finally arrives.
